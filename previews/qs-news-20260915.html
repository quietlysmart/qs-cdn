How much of your business can AI handle reliably?

AI use is not a switch. A business can use a chatbot every day and still have no repeatable AI work. Another can have one small job that runs well because its instructions, information and checks are written down. Use these six stages on one recurring task, not the business as a whole.

1. No AI

You do the job yourself from memory, old emails or a blank document.

2. One-off help

You ask AI for help when the task appears. It may save time, but you start again and the result depends on what you remember to say.

3. Written instructions

You have written down the voice, facts or steps the job needs. AI can follow a clearer brief, but you still move work between tools yourself.

4. One repeatable task

One job has a repeatable prompt or instruction, a known input and a useful result. You still check each run, but the process is no longer invented from scratch.

5. Connected context and checks

The task can use the right business records and tools. It has checks for missing information, failed steps and work that needs your decision.

6. Limited unattended work

The task can start and finish parts of the job without a fresh prompt. Important decisions still wait for you, and a future run has to prove it can recover when something goes wrong.

My meeting system sat between stages five and six. The rules now keep its records and give each meeting one record, but I am not calling it automatic until an unattended run works properly. A manual success tells you the idea works. It does not prove the system will keep working.

Your 30-minute check

Choose one recurring job and write one page. Ask four questions: 1. What starts the job? 2. What information does it need? 3. What does a good result look like? 4. What still needs my approval? Then write down the stage you reached and the next gap. That page gives your AI something specific to work with, and it shows you what still needs a human check.

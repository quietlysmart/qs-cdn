Where your business sits on the AI ladder

Some owners do every job by hand. Some ask a chatbot when stuck. Others have written down how the business speaks, what a good quote looks like, or how a weekly report should be checked. Some connect those instructions to their tools. Two businesses can both say they use AI while working at different stages.

The six-stage check

Think of six stages. Stage one is no AI. Stage two is one-off help. Stage three is written voice and instructions. Stage four is one repeatable task with clear steps and AI support. Stage five connects those instructions to your information, tools and checks. Stage six runs parts without prompting, while important decisions still wait for approval.

Choose one recurring task. Find the highest stage it reaches without you filling the gaps. No AI is one. One-off prompts are two. Written rules are three. A repeatable AI-assisted task is four. Connected tools and checks are five. Work that runs by itself, with approval at important points, is six. Write down the stage and the next gap. That is more useful than saying you use AI.

Most owners sit between stages. That is normal. The question is whether AI knows enough about your work to do the next part properly.

The business memory test

My meeting system was partly built, but unfinished. The system that reads messages failed. Its filter would also have ignored the invitation, calendar confirmation and call recap. I changed the rules so those records stay, the system tries again after a temporary error, and each meeting gets one record. A manual pass found context for an upcoming PRISM session, made its brief and recognised an earlier recap. One future run still needs to work without help before I call it automatic.

You can do this without building anything. Spend 30 minutes writing one page for a recurring task: “What does my business need this task to know?” Write what starts it, what it may use, what good looks like and what needs approval. Ask your AI where the page is blank. That is your next step.

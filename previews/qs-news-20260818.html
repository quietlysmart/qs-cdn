Map the task before AI touches it

A few weeks ago, I sat with a team at a private members’ club and looked at where daily work got stuck. They weren’t asking for a clever chatbot. They wanted better use of member information, more relevant communications, a smoother new-member experience and clearer reports for the people running the club.

It’s tempting to reach for a tool. I’ve learned to resist it. Before you automate anything, see the work as it happens.

Follow the handoff

We mapped the chain in plain language. Member behaviour is gathered. Someone makes sense of it. A message is prepared. Member preferences are captured. The results feed back into reporting.

Each step has a person, a decision and a handoff. Work gets delayed, duplicated or quietly dropped at those points.

The map changed the problem. A broad request for AI became a sequence we could tackle in stages, with customer records and the main workflow at the centre. That gave us a better starting point than choosing an app and forcing the business around it.

Write one real job

Most owners carry this map in their heads. That makes it easy to underestimate. You know who checks the inbox, which spreadsheet gets updated, what needs approval and what happens when someone is away. The business runs on that knowledge, but nobody else can see it clearly.

Write one real job as a short account. Start when the request arrives. End when the customer, colleague or owner has what they need. Include the awkward bits: the exception, missing information, approval and last-minute change.

If you leave those out, future automation will look neat and work badly. Only then can AI fit the process. It might draft a message, sort incoming information or prepare a first report. Decide where it may act, where it must pause and who checks the result.

Then you can see whether it saves time or simply moves the same confusion into a new screen. The point isn’t a perfect diagram. It’s making one repeatable task visible enough for a tool to help.

This week, spend an hour mapping one task by hand before automating a minute of it. Pick a job that happens often. Write the steps in order and mark every handoff.
